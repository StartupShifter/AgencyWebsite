# Service scope guide

The owner confirmed these ten service areas and describes a $1,000 default one-time engagement covering the listed services, with a flexible price as services are added or removed. **Exact features, quantities, and boundaries at that price remain undefined.** The scope items below are possibilities to define for each client, not automatic unlimited inclusions. Specify setup versus ongoing management, deliverables and quantities, client inputs, dependencies, exclusions, price factors, and timing before sale. See [company-context.md](company-context.md) for pricing and publication rules and [questions.md](questions.md) for open decisions.

## 1. Ecommerce websites and applications

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Help a business sell online through an ecommerce website or application. The agency builds on Shopify, WordPress + WooCommerce, or another suitable platform or website builder **subject to support confirmation**. “Woo” is shorthand for WooCommerce, not a separate platform.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Requirements and platform recommendation; store structure and navigation; theme selection/customization; agreed custom design or development; products, variants, collections/categories, and catalog organization; cart and checkout configuration within platform capabilities; payment and shipping setup; relevant system integrations; mobile usability; testing, launch, and handover.
- **Define the engagement:** Identify the one-time build scope and separately agreed ongoing store management, if any. The client may need to provide product data, imagery, copy, policies, tax/shipping rules, accounts, payment approval, and timely reviews.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Catalog size and data quality, design depth, platform constraints, custom features, integrations, migrations, localization, third-party approvals, and review cycles. Confirm gateway availability, checkout limits, licenses, hosting/subscriptions, ownership, and support. Do not imply native mobile apps, unlimited customization, every gateway, migrations, multilingual stores, or every platform are included or supported.
- **Website explanation:** Describe the chosen platform and store outcomes in plain language; show exact build inclusions and optional integrations, catalog limits, launch steps, and third-party costs.

## 2. Social media and campaign management

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Help clients maintain and manage social media campaigns and a consistent presence.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Strategy/planning, content calendar, agreed content creation, posting and scheduling, organic campaign management, monitoring, reporting, and optimization. Define networks, formats, quantities, approvals, and whether community management or paid advertising is included.
- **Define the engagement:** Separate one-time account/campaign setup from any ongoing management and state its monthly coverage and charge. The client may need to provide account access, brand material, product information, legal approvals, and a contact for timely content approval.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Network count, post and asset volume, production effort, ad complexity, reporting frequency, and approval turnaround. Agency management fees and advertising spend are separate. Do not include photography, video production, influencer management, customer support, or paid media buying unless quoted.
- **Website explanation:** Name supported networks only when confirmed; show cadence, deliverable counts, review flow, organic versus paid scope, and what reports contain.

## 3. Chatbots and customer interaction automation

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Help answer suitable customer questions or route interactions through chatbot-related services and management.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Use-case design; supported-channel setup; approved knowledge sources; question/action boundaries; store or business-data integrations; human handoff; testing and review; ongoing updates and maintenance.
- **Define the engagement:** Separate initial design/setup from ongoing operation and state its monthly coverage and charge. The client may need to provide policies, FAQs, approved knowledge, channel accounts, escalation contacts, data access, and review of responses.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Channel support, conversation volume, knowledge quality, action complexity, integration access, testing needs, subscriptions, and usage limits. Agree on privacy, escalation, error handling, and responsibility for content updates. Do not promise perfect answers, unlimited conversations, fully autonomous support, or an unconfirmed channel.
- **Website explanation:** Give concrete approved use cases and human handoff; explain what the bot may do, where it runs, what it depends on, and which software/usage charges are separate.

## 4. Inventory management systems

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Give a business a clearer, more reliable view of products and stock across locations or channels. Inventory solutions and related management are offered.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Selection/configuration of an existing tool, custom system work if separately agreed, connections between systems, initial imports, stock sync, low-stock workflows, reports, and ongoing administration/support.
- **Define the engagement:** Specify tool setup, custom build, system integration, and any separately charged ongoing administration. The client may need to provide SKU/variant data, stock locations, warehouse processes, source-of-truth rules, access, and accurate starting counts.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Product and variant counts, warehouses, channels, import quality, sync frequency, exception handling, and reporting depth. Agree who owns stock accuracy and reconciles discrepancies. Custom software development and daily stock administration are not included in every engagement.
- **Website explanation:** Separate “configure,” “connect,” “build,” and “manage”; describe the actual systems, stock workflows, data responsibilities, and support limits in a proposal.

## 5. Shipping and order operations

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Reduce manual steps and errors between orders, shipping tools, and inventory workflows.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Shipping-provider integrations, rules and rates, order routing, tracking, label/dispatch workflows where supported, returns workflows where agreed, and store/shipping/inventory connections.
- **Define the engagement:** Separate one-time software/workflow setup from any ongoing workflow management and its monthly charge. The client may need to supply courier accounts, negotiated rates, packaging details, shipping zones, fulfillment rules, return policies, and operational staff contacts.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Provider capabilities, zones and rate rules, order volume, multiple locations, workflow exceptions, and integration quality. Agree who owns live orders and provider disputes. Physical warehousing, packing, dispatching, delivery, and courier dispute handling are **not confirmed services**.
- **Website explanation:** State the software and workflow functions explicitly; do not use “fulfillment” to imply physical handling. Separate provider and shipping charges from agency fees.

## 6. ERP and CRM integrations

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Connect business information between ERP, CRM, store, and other systems to reduce disconnected or repetitive work.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Connector selection/configuration, agreed custom integration, field mapping, data preparation, sync rules, error handling, monitoring, testing, and maintenance.
- **Define the engagement:** Separate one-time integration from ongoing monitoring/support and its monthly charge. The client may need to provide exact systems and versions, API credentials through a secure channel, documentation, data owners, sample records, and access to technical contacts.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Records and fields, source/destination, one-way or two-way flow, frequency, data cleanup, existing connectors versus custom APIs, platform restrictions, and exception volume. Confirm access, rate limits, responsibility for bad source data, security, and maintenance. Compatibility with every ERP or CRM is not promised; every named integration needs verification.
- **Website explanation:** Describe the business flow and systems in everyday terms, then distinguish verified connectors from proposed custom work and state monitoring/support coverage.

## 7. Maintenance and ongoing technical support

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Keep agreed websites and systems maintained and provide technical help after launch.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Routine checks, applicable updates and compatibility checks, bug fixes, monitoring, backup/recovery tasks where agreed, and support handling.
- **Define the engagement:** Maintenance requested as an ongoing service carries a separate monthly charge based on chosen coverage. Specify covered systems, included capacity, support channel/hours, response targets, escalation, and work billed separately. The client may need to maintain licenses/accounts, provide access, report issues with evidence, and approve disruptive changes.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Platform mix, site complexity, update risk, incident volume, coverage hours, recovery requirements, and legacy issues. Distinguish maintenance, defect correction, and new feature development. Do not imply 24/7 coverage, unlimited changes, resolution guarantees, or an SLA without approval.
- **Website explanation:** Publish a simple coverage table once approved: tasks, capacity, hours, response method, exclusions, and how new work is quoted.

## 8. Performance monitoring and improvement

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Find and address agreed performance bottlenecks in websites or connected systems.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Baseline assessment, bottleneck findings, prioritized recommendations, agreed optimizations, monitoring, and reporting.
- **Define the engagement:** Separate one-time audit/optimization from any ongoing measurement and its monthly charge. The client may need to provide analytics, hosting/platform access, business priorities, representative traffic data, and approval for changes.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Existing architecture, third-party scripts/apps, data quality, traffic patterns, platform limits, and target metrics. Define baseline, measurement method, scope, and agreed targets before any specific commitment. Do not promise perfect scores, zero downtime, guaranteed conversion gains, or results outside agency control.
- **Website explanation:** Show how assessment leads to prioritized work and reportable measures; label targets as project-specific, not universal guarantees.

## 9. Research, themes, and assets

- **Business need and confirmed service:** Inform project choices through in-depth engagement research and suitable creative direction, themes, and assets. Research and creative direction are related but distinct work. The owner has now specified company, market, and competitor research plus a basic Figma design draft for the initial handoff.
- **Potential scoped deliverables:** Business/product understanding; competitor, market, or customer research; platform/feature recommendations; theme recommendations; visual references; design direction; selected or created assets; presentations and recommendations.
- **Define the engagement:** Discovery-only project, initial project phase, ongoing research/creative support, or a combination. The client may need to provide product knowledge, existing brand files, customer insight, access to stakeholders, and approvals.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Research depth, number of competitors/interviews, quality of inputs, rounds of exploration, original creation versus selection, and licensing. Asset creation, asset selection, and paid licenses are separate responsibilities. Do not imply unlimited artwork, photography, copywriting, premium themes, or third-party licenses.
- **Website explanation:** Describe research outputs and creative outputs separately, with quantity/depth, sources, review stages, and licensing costs made clear.

## 10. Client presentations and implementation planning

- **Business need and confirmed intention:** Give a newly onboarded client an early, shared view of the project and design direction. Onboarding follows a signed shared contract, 40% advance, and company/project details; the client joins an onboarding group.
- **Confirmed initial handoff:** Company, market-segment, and competitor research plus a basic Figma design draft with variants so the client can select a direction and request revisions. A presentation may communicate these outputs; its exact format is TBD. Further roadmap material is proposed, not approved.
- **Define the engagement:** Clarify the group platform, minimum usable company details, research depth, number of design variants and revision rounds, and whether later planning updates are included. The client supplies the agreed inputs and feedback.
- **Price/timing drivers and boundaries:** Project complexity, input quality, research depth, and design/review effort. Earlier owner wording said “within 24 hours”; the latest said “after 24 hours.” The clock start after prerequisites, elapsed versus business hours, timezone, complex-project treatment, and start-of-implementation approval are unresolved. This does not mean the website, all assets/research, or integrations are finished in 24 hours.
- **Website explanation:** Describe the research and Figma draft as the intended early handoff; do not publish an exact deadline or revision allowance until the remaining conditions are approved.

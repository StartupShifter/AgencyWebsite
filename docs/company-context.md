## Proposed information architecture

The recommended pages are Home, Services overview, detailed service pages, Services and pricing, Process and onboarding, Work or case studies, About, Contact or project brief, FAQs, and relevant policy pages. The owner-approved Basic/Pro comparison can appear on the homepage; additional plans need owner confirmation. The home page should quickly explain who the agency helps, the outcomes it works toward, service groups, genuine work evidence when supplied, pricing guidance, delivery stages, the onboarding research/design draft, separately priced ongoing support, and a clear next action. Use short summaries that lead to detailed pages rather than a single overwhelming page. Each service page should explain the problem, possible scope, what is selected for a specific project, limits, dependencies, client responsibilities, and price factors. Distinguish **available service**, **chosen one-time scope**, **separately quoted work**, and **monthly ongoing service**.

Use only verifiable portfolio examples and approved permissions. No client names, logos, testimonials, results, certifications, awards, partner badges, or statistics may be fabricated. If proof is unavailable, describe the process and scope honestly instead.

## Writing and decision rules

Use specific, client-friendly language and explain technical terms when needed. Connect each service to a business need, then show what is selected, what can be added, and what the client supplies. Avoid “we handle everything,” universal compatibility, guaranteed outcomes, and unbounded support promises. Preserve confirmed facts; label recommendations and assumptions in internal work. The confirmed plan prices and advance may be stated with their limits, but unresolved scope and contractual details require owner input before publication. Update this file when the owner explicitly confirms or changes a policy; move answered items from [questions.md](questions.md) into the appropriate confirmed section and note any remaining conflict.
